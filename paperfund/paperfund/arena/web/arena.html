Fund Arena

Ten funds trade the same exchange for five years: HFT market makers, a latency sniper, a pod shop, a trend CTA, value and levered funds, the MiroFish paper fund from PR #28, and a small fund built on your market-data skills. Every number on this page comes from that simulation ( alternate histories), except the real-fund table near the end.

Simulation only. Rival funds are archetypes, not real firms, and their running costs are estimates. The order book is simulated microsecond by microsecond for days and those days are replayed by market regime across the five years.

Tape 2

Why the fastest firm gets filled first

Exchanges fill resting orders by price, then by arrival time. When fair value moves, every resting quote at the old price is suddenly a gift to whoever takes it, and the new best price has an empty queue. Two races follow: snipers race makers to the stale quotes, and makers race each other to the front of the new queue.

These are two real moments from the simulated book, slowed down a million times. Each coloured block is one firm's order; its position in the row is its place in the queue.

Why some firms choose high risk

  • The manager's payoff is lopsided. With 2% and 20% fees the manager keeps a fifth of the upside and none of the downside. Big Bet Capital earned in fees while its investors lost .
  • Investors pay for a story. A 30% year attracts money; a steady 6% does not. That pulls managers toward leverage until a margin call, which is what happened to Big Bet on day .
  • Low-risk firms need a real edge per trade. Apex and the sniper run tiny risk (a worst day of ) because they make thousands of small, positive bets a day. You can only be low-risk and profitable if the edge is real.
