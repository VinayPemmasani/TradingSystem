    What is actually missing

    Statistics you can bet on

    Distributions, t-stats, information coefficient, multiple testing. The paper fund's tuning picked a setting with in-sample Sharpe +0.43 that went to −2.81 out of sample. Spotting that before launch is the whole job.

    Research method

    Hypothesis first, out-of-sample tests, placebo tests, no look-ahead. The paper fund's real news did no better than news shifted twenty days late (−$437.6M against −$443.3M). That single test is worth more than any feed handler optimisation.

    Portfolio and risk

    Sizing, volatility targeting, correlation, drawdown budgets. The fundamental sleeve lost 37% with no risk budget. Module 9 replays it with a volatility target and a drawdown cut: −$86.8M instead of −$331.6M.

    Execution and capacity

    Costs, market impact, how big a strategy can get. The trend sleeve made +3.15% at $10M and −0.18% at $1.5bn. Same signal; the market impact ate it.

    Finance basics

    PnL attribution, marks, Sharpe, drawdown, benchmark. You already reconcile fills; the step is asking why the PnL is what it is.

    Python research stack

    numpy, pandas, a notebook habit and plotting. Your C++ is a strength; the research loop is faster in Python and you move the winner to C++.

    Part 2

    Where fifty feeds are an advantage

    Most quants learned markets from bar data and vendor files. You know what the data is before it becomes a bar. These six advantages are real, and the course builds on each one.

    You can tell a signal from a bug

    Most research blow-ups are data errors that look like alpha. MD-414's auction-day volume bug creates fake volume spikes that a volume strategy would trade. You find that in minutes; the quant in the duel solves it 46% of the time.

    Module 10

    You know which clock is which

    Exchange, gateway, receive and vendor timestamps differ by microseconds to seconds. Look-ahead bias usually hides there. You can build backtests that cannot see the future.

    Module 7

    You know how fills really happen

    Price-time priority, queue position, replace rules, self-match prevention, auctions, each venue's quirks. Backtests that assume you get filled at the mid are wrong, and you can model the real thing.

    Module 3

    Cross-venue and cross-asset structure

    Futures lead ETFs, ETFs lead stocks, FX fixes move at known times, auctions differ by exchange. Lead-lag and auction strategies need exactly the multi-feed view you have.

    Capstone

    Features nobody else can see

    Add and cancel rates, odd lots, hidden fills, queue depletion speed. They come only from raw order-by-order feeds, and you already parse them.

    Module 2

    Research to production with no rewrite

    Quant teams lose months moving research into a low-latency system. You can do it in weeks, which is why the co-located HFT sleeve swung from −9.18% to +11.02%.

    No top degree needed

    What gets a market data developer hired into research is a public, honest track record: two or three write-ups with out-of-sample results and placebo tests, a Kaggle market-microstructure competition (Optiver's closing auction one and Jane Street's real-time forecasting one both use order book data), and the production skills you already have. Firms test what you can do in the interview; a portfolio of real research is the strongest answer.

    Reading list, in order

    Larry Harris, Trading and Exchanges (you know half of it already). Ernest Chan, Quantitative Trading. Cartea, Jaimungal and Penalva, Algorithmic and High-Frequency Trading. Grinold and Kahn, Active Portfolio Management. Marcos López de Prado, Advances in Financial Machine Learning, chapters on backtest overfitting. MIT OpenCourseWare 18.S096 for the maths.

    The spread of outcomes

    20,000 runs in which each problem is solved or not by chance. The veteran's outcome is narrow. The quant's is wide because most of the value sits in two or three research calls.

    Is paying a quant more justified?

    What the market pays

    RoleReported pay
    Quant developer joining a hedge fund$250k–$400k total (eFinancialCareers, quoting Balyasny)
    Senior engineer, Citadel job ads$150k–$300k base for 6+ years (Tradermath)
    Quant researcher, base$180k–$250k, median $213k (Tradermath)
    Quant researcher, bonus80–200% of base (Recruiting from Scratch)
    Senior researcher with a strong year£1.5M–£2M reported (Young and Calculated)

    Public surveys and job ads from 2025–26; check current figures before you negotiate.

    Why the gap is mostly bonus

    Base pay for a strong engineer and a researcher is close. The difference is the bonus, and the bonus is a share of the PnL the researcher made. If the researcher made nothing, the bonus is small or the job ends. So the premium pays for upside the firm only gives away after it has earned it.

    The veteran's value is real, but most of it is losses avoided, and that is capped by how big incidents get. The quant's value grows with every dollar of capital, and it can be zero. That is why firms pay the quant more on average and fire more of them.

    What this means for you

    • At a large systematic fund, a veteran who stays a pure market data developer is valued like a senior engineer: well paid, with a ceiling.
    • At an HFT or market-making shop, or a fund under about $200M, your profile is already worth more than the quant's in this model, because speed, fills and data quality are the edge there.
    • The veteran after the course is worth about four times the veteran today in the model at $5bn, and level with the quant at about $450M. Pairing with a researcher beats either alone at every size.
